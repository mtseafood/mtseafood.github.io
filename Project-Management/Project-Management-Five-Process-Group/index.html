Project Management - Five Process Group

Posted on 2025-04-25 Edited on 2025-05-03 In Project Management Views:
Five Process Group

Five Process Group

The five main processes are initiation, planning, execution, monitoring, and closure. The following events occur in the five main processes:

No Process Group Description
1 Initiating Define and authorize the project. Identify the Business Case.
2 Planning Define and refine goals, plan action steps.
3 Executing Integrate manpower and resources to carry out the project.
4 Monitoring and Controlling Regularly measure and monitor progress to control the project and achieve goals.
5 Closing Ensure that the products, services, or results of the project are accepted, and orderly close the project.

Project Life Cycle

In the early stages of a project, stakeholders have a significant influence on the direction of the project, and the cost of changes is relatively low. However, as the project progresses to later stages, stakeholders’ ability to influence decreases, and the cost of changes increases. In simple terms, this is the concept of “the ship has sailed.”

It’s important to note that a decrease in influence over the project’s direction does not mean a decrease in influence over the project’s success or failure. Discontented individuals can still easily cause the project to fail.

Common Tools

Goal Setting

Having goals provides direction; without goals or with too many goals, it is difficult to reach a consensus. Goals should be limited to three, prioritized accordingly, and must meet the SMART criteria. Many people say that leisure is a goal, but leisure can serve various purposes such as resting, experiencing different lifestyles, connecting with others, and meeting new people, etc. Different goals will generate different action plans.

Business Case

All projects must be cost-effective. Are the products, services, and results generated by the project worth our time and effort?

Common Misconceptions

Goals cannot be changed

As long as the sponsor agrees, goals can be changed. Of course, each time a goal is modified, the team will need some time to refocus, which is something to be mindful of.

Plans cannot change

If we could control the weather and prevent illness, then plans could certainly remain unchanged. Unfortunately, we are neither gods nor can we control the environment. When circumstances change, it is natural to adjust plans. Just like when the wind changes direction, if we do not adjust the sails, will we reach our destination?

However, changing plans can be painful, especially after a lot of preparation and processes have been set. This is why agile project management is becoming popular in frequently changing environments. Agile methods emphasize short iterations to avoid spending excessive time on plans that are bound to change. Nevertheless, it is still necessary to set mid-to-long-term goals to serve as a direction.

Roles in Project Management

In the framework of software management, there are four roles in running a project:

1. Project Sponsor

Quantity: One
The person who funds the project, usually a senior manager, holds political responsibility for the project’s success or failure.

2. Project Manager (PM)

Quantity: One
The person who actually runs and monitors the project, holding direct responsibility for its success or failure.

3. Project Team

Quantity: Several to many
Those who participate in the project.

4. Stakeholders

Quantity: A truckload to countless
Everyone who has a say in the project.

4 Steps to Create a Project Stakeholder Analysis Diagram

Identifying and managing your project stakeholders is an excellent way to lay the foundation for a successful project. When key project stakeholders trust you, they are more likely to provide support, which can be crucial throughout the project lifecycle. Conversely, without supportive stakeholders, you may find yourself having to set stakeholder expectations halfway through the project, which can lead to changes and unnecessary project risks. To avoid this, you must take the following four steps to create an effective project stakeholder analysis diagram.

Step 1: Identify Project Stakeholders

Before managing stakeholder expectations, you first need to know who the stakeholders are. Be sure to include both internal and external stakeholders. To figure out who your project stakeholders are, ask yourself:

  • Who cares about this project?
  • Who will be affected by this project?
  • Who can influence this project?
  • Who can approve/reject this project?

Step 2: Determine the Influence and Interest Levels of Project Stakeholders

If high-level project stakeholders participate, they can truly elevate your project to a higher level. The best way to confirm high-level involvement is to establish a clear stakeholder analysis diagram that outlines the influence and interest levels of each project stakeholder. This matrix structure (sometimes referred to as the power-interest matrix) is the best way to visually represent the four main groups of project stakeholders.

The four main groups of project stakeholders are:

  1. High Influence and High Interest: Manage Closely
  2. High Influence and Low Interest: Keep Satisfied
  3. Low Influence and High Interest: Keep Informed
  4. Low Influence and Low Interest: Monitor

Step 3: Understand Stakeholder Needs

The reality is that some stakeholders may disagree with specific elements of the project. As a project manager, you are responsible for understanding their needs and perspectives, and finding solutions that will not negatively impact the success of the project. You are not obligated to do everything that stakeholders ask of you, but you are obligated to listen to and understand their needs.

Sometimes, certain individuals may seem like “difficult stakeholders,” but in fact, their priorities may simply differ from yours. Your work might even interrupt parts of their workflow. If you try to see things from their perspective, you will have a better chance of finding solutions and turning the situation into a win-win outcome.

When in doubt, put yourself in others’ shoes. Ask yourself:

  • What do they need?
  • What level of communication do they want?
  • What is the most effective communication strategy?
  • Do these stakeholders have anyone they influence, or are they influenced by others?
  • How do you accurately identify the interests of stakeholders?

Step 4: Include Project Stakeholders in the Recipient List

Once you have identified your project stakeholders and considered their needs, it is essential to invite them to participate in project planning workshops and kickoff meetings (if applicable). Key project stakeholders should also sign off on the project charter, project plan, project objectives, and project scope.

Common Myths

  1. Every stakeholder needs to be satisfied.
    Stakeholder management involves categorization, allowing us to focus our efforts where they matter most and avoid the dilemma of trying to please everyone.

  2. Stakeholders only need to be dealt with.
    Valuable insights and details that can significantly impact the success or failure of a project can be gained from stakeholders. Properly utilizing these insights can lead to smoother project execution.
